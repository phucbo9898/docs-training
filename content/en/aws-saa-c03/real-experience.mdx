This page records the study journey of a team member who passed the exam, so everyone has realistic expectations and avoids the same mistakes.

## You don't need high practice scores to pass

<Callout type="info" title="Realistic expectations for exam-mode scores">
This member's practice exam scores ranged from 35% to 50% in exam mode, yet they passed the real exam. Practice exams are generally considered slightly harder than the real one. What matters more than the score is an **upward trend** and **fixing recurring mistakes**.
</Callout>

## The most effective study methods

1. **Group keywords by domain, not by exam.** Notes per exam get long and repetitive. Grouping by domain keeps them short and shows your weak spots right away.
2. **Write keywords as "seen in the question → choose service"** rather than writing definitions.
3. **Study with classification questions.** Many mistakes look like "last time I picked A and was wrong, this time I picked B, but this question actually needed A". The wavering only stops once you have a clear criterion to tell them apart (see [Quick classification questions](/en/aws-saa-c03/classification-questions)).
4. **Practice the reverse scenario too.** After getting a question wrong, find a scenario where the "trap" answer is actually the right one. Being able to answer both ways is real understanding.
5. **Redo wrong questions until none are left**, and only then move on to a new exam.

## The most repeated mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Three common mistakes">

- Picking a **stronger option just to be safe** (multi-region when Multi-AZ is enough, Warm standby when the RTO allows hours, 6 instances per AZ when 3 is enough). The exam rewards the option that is **just enough**.
- **Missing a number or condition** in the question: RPO of 1 minute but choosing hourly snapshots, a job running 20 minutes but choosing Lambda.
- **Choosing based on a recently learned keyword** without reading the conditions of the current question.

</Callout>

## Tips for the exam room

<Callout type="tip" title="In the exam room">

1. **Read the final question and the conditions first**, jot them down on scratch paper (_20 minutes, RPO 1 minute, compliance, LEAST overhead…_), then read the scenario.
2. **Check each answer against every condition.** Violating a single condition rules it out.
3. **"Select TWO/THREE" questions:** the answers must complement each other; don't pick two answers that do the same job.
4. **When two answers both seem reasonable:** pick the one that matches more conditions with fewer unnecessary components; with _LEAST overhead_, prefer managed services.
5. **Use all the time.** 130 minutes for 65 questions is enough to read carefully and revisit flagged questions. Don't submit early.

</Callout>
